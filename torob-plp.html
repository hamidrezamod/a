01 — Problem
When searching for car parts, users do not always search for a specific brand or model. Queries can be as broad as “Peugeot 405 clutch kit,” which can lead to a large number of results. The challenge was to help users find a suitable product and a reliable seller faster. Design goal: Make it easier for users to find the right product and seller within the PLP.
02 — User Persona
Based on the user data provided by Torob, I created a representative persona to better understand the user's context, goals, needs, and pain points.
03 — User Needs & Pain Points
Using the user data provided by Torob, I identified the main needs and pain points related to the shopping experience and the PLP. I then grouped them under four relevant heuristic categories: Security, Trust, Relevance, and Orientation.
04 — From User Needs to Existing Capabilities
I reviewed Torob's existing features, widgets, and data and mapped them to the same four categories. I then identified which capabilities could address the user needs found in the previous step.
05 — UX Redesign
Not all of the identified features and data were currently available in the PLP, so I explored how they could be integrated into the product card and the overall PLP experience. Key changes included improving product information, adding a Compatible Cars filter, improving filter accessibility and seller location information, and giving more emphasis to relevant products and reliable sellers. Since the searched car model does not always represent the user's own vehicle, the Compatible Cars filter allows users to select their vehicle and see products that sellers have identified as compatible with it.
06 — Wireframing
To accommodate more product information without making the card feel too dense, I changed the product card from a vertical layout to a horizontal one. The product actions were also reorganized into a single column to create more space for product information.
07 — Final Design
The final design brought the key information and features identified throughout the process into the PLP, with the goal of helping users make a faster and more confident purchase decision. Throughout the UI design, I tried to stay consistent with Torob's existing design system and make the solution scalable to other product categories.
08 — Evaluation
Since this project was created as a design challenge and was not tested with real users, the evaluation phase was defined as a validation plan rather than a conducted test. The proposed solution would be evaluated through usability testing to understand whether users can find a suitable product more efficiently using the redesigned filters and product listing structure. We would also examine whether product information, pricing, and seller details are clear and useful for making a purchase decision. Key metrics could include time to find a suitable product, number of interactions or filter changes, task completion rate, and user satisfaction. These findings would help identify whether the filtering logic, information hierarchy, or prioritization of products and sellers needs further refinement.
