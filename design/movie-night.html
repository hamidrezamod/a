Problem
When people want to watch a movie together, choosing what to watch can take a significant amount of time. They often have several options, but still don't know which movie fits their mood at that moment. They need to check genres, ratings, reviews, and story summaries to understand whether a movie is right for them. This becomes more frustrating when several people are involved, because everyone may have a different preference. As a result, a large part of their limited leisure time can be spent looking for a movie instead of watching one.
Goal
Help users find a high-quality movie that matches their current mood, faster and with less decision-making effort.
Persona
To better understand the problem, I created a persona based on the data collected from different users. This helped define a representative user and keep their needs, behaviors, and goals at the center of the design process.
Pain Points & Needs
I grouped the pain points and needs identified from the user data to better understand the main challenges users face when choosing a movie. This helped turn scattered observations into clear design opportunities.
Recommendation Concept
The core idea was to create a movie assistant that recommends films based on how the user wants to feel, rather than relying on traditional genres or familiar topics. To achieve this, I designed a system that uses AI to analyze movie data, along with audience and critic reviews, and map each film to different emotions and moods. This allows the system to understand not only what a movie is about, but also what kind of feeling it can create and when it might be suitable to watch. After watching a recommended film, users can provide feedback on how well it matched their selected mood. This feedback can be used to improve the AI's classification and gradually build a better understanding of each user's preferences.
User Flow
I mapped the user's journey from selecting their desired mood to receiving and watching a movie recommendation. The flow was designed to keep the decision-making process simple while allowing users to refine their choice when needed.
Wireframes
I translated the user flow and core requirements into low-fidelity wireframes to define the structure, hierarchy, and interactions before moving into visual design.
Final Design
Evaluation
Since the product was not implemented, the evaluation phase was planned rather than conducted. The system could be evaluated through both user feedback and behavioral data. After watching a recommended film, users would rate how well the movie matched the mood they had selected. We would also track how many recommendations users go through before choosing a movie and how often they change their selected mood. These signals would help identify where the recommendation experience needs improvement. For example, frequent mood changes or a high number of skipped recommendations could indicate that the mood classification or the ranking of movies within each mood needs to be refined. User feedback would provide an additional qualitative signal to validate whether the recommendations actually matched the intended feeling.
